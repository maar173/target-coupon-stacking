# Target coupons: how to stack Circle offers, the 5% Circle Card discount and cashback on one order

Search "Target coupons" and you'll get a wall of pages promising 25% off or 50% off with a code. Most of those codes don't exist as codes. Target's discounts live somewhere else — inside the Target app and the free Circle program — and the way they stack is what actually decides whether you save 5% or 30% on an order.

This guide covers where real Target offers come from, the stacking rules Target actually publishes, what each paid membership costs, and how a cashback layer fits on top without breaking the tracking.

## Target rarely rewards you for hunting for codes

Target does issue promo codes from time to time, but the everyday discounts aren't code-based. They're **offers you clip to your account** before checkout, and they apply automatically once you're signed in. A "Target 30% off code" page is usually pointing at a Circle offer, a seasonal sale, or a gift-card promotion rather than a code you'd type into a box.

That distinction matters because it changes the whole approach. Instead of collecting codes, you collect offers:

- **Target Circle offers** — category or item-level coupons in the app and on the site. Clip them, and they come off at checkout in store or online.
- **The weekly ad and Target's deals page** — sale prices, not coupons. No clipping needed, but they expire weekly, usually within one to two weeks.
- **Manufacturer coupons** — paper ones from the Sunday insert plus digital ones in the app. These are the only coupons that function like classic coupons.
- **Gift-card promotions** — "buy three household items, get a $10 gift card" style deals. The gift card isn't a discount on the current order, which is why people undercount them.
- **Member events** — Target's fall Circle Deal Days is dated Oct. 6–7 this year, with up to 40% off across home, beauty, toys and tech, and Circle 360 members shopping select deals a day early on Oct. 5.

One practical detail worth knowing: in store, open the app and let the cashier scan the wallet barcode. That applies every clipped offer in your account in one go, which is faster and less error-prone than hoping you remembered to clip everything.

There are also coupons you get once and forget:

- **Birthday offer.** Circle members get a 5% off coupon for a single transaction during their birthday month.
- **Registry completion discount.** Baby and wedding registries come with a 15% off coupon for buying remaining items from your list.
- **Student verification.** Target's own instructions: log into your account, go to settings, find the "I'm a student" box, and complete verification. Once verified, student-only offers and pricing show up, including the discounted Circle 360 rate.

## The stacking rule that does most of the work

Target allows **up to three different types of offers on a single item**. Per item, the combination can be:

> One manufacturer coupon (paper or digital), plus one Target Circle category offer, plus one item-specific Circle offer.

You can't stack two manufacturer coupons on the same item, and you can't stack two category offers. Once you know the shape of the rule, deal-building becomes arithmetic rather than guesswork.

Here's a made-up order to show the mechanics, not a real promotion:

- A $100 apparel item on sale for $80.
- A Circle category offer for $10 off a $50 clothing purchase.
- A $5 item-specific Circle coupon for that brand.
- Paid with the Circle Card, which takes 5% off the remainder.

$80 minus $10 minus $5 is $65, and 5% off that lands you near $61.75 before tax. The same item bought without clipping anything costs $80, and without the card it costs $65. That's the gap between someone who "uses Target coupons" and someone who stacks.

## Is Target Circle 360 worth the money?

The honest version is that 360 is a delivery membership wearing a loyalty-program costume. Everything most shoppers think of as "Target rewards" — the 1% back, the offers, the birthday coupon — is in the free tier.

What the $99 buys:

- Unlimited same-day delivery on orders of $35 or more, fulfilled through Shipt, often within a couple of hours. Non-members pay $9.99 per same-day order.
- Free 2-day shipping on eligible items.
- An extra 30 days on returns beyond the standard policy.
- Monthly freebies chosen on the first of the month, plus early access to sale events.

Target's own marketing puts the potential savings at over $300 a year, based on two same-day orders a month plus a standalone Shipt membership. That math depends entirely on whether you'd otherwise order same-day twice a month. If you would, the $49 Circle Card rate makes it a straightforward call. If you live outside a same-day delivery zone, the headline perk is dead weight and the free shipping alone rarely justifies $99.

Two things to check before you commit:

1. **The 14-day free trial.** It's a real trial, but with limited benefits, so don't judge the full membership by it.
2. **Membership fees don't earn cashback.** Paying $99 for 360 isn't a purchase that a cashback portal will reward. The cashback comes from the orders you place, not the subscription.

ShopBack's own comparison of Target Circle 360, Walmart+ and Amazon Prime lands on roughly the same conclusion: the membership pays for itself if Target is your primary store, and doesn't if it's a backup.

## Mistakes that quietly cost money

- **Clipping after checkout.** Offers have to be on your account before the transaction. There's no retroactive clipping.
- **Using two Circle offers on one item.** One category offer plus one item-specific offer is the ceiling.
- **Treating a gift-card promo as a discount.** A $10 gift card on a $36 purchase is a future $10, not a current $10, and gift cards don't earn cashback either.
- **Forgetting the 14-day price adjustment window.** If something you bought drops in price, Target will adjust with proof of purchase inside 14 days.
- **Assuming the 5% and Circle offers conflict.** They don't. The Circle Card's 5% applies on top of existing Circle deals — that's the whole reason the card exists.
